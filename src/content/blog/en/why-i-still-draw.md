I did not begin drawing with a grand plan. I started with basic forms because they were the foundation: cubes, spheres, cylinders, proportion, light, and shadow. I took classes for basic forms and statue drawing because fundamentals deserve correct instruction.

After that, most of my learning was self-directed. I moved from watercolour to acrylic, and later to oil painting. Each medium changed the speed at which I had to look. Watercolour asks for acceptance. Acrylic allows revision. Oil stays open longer and makes patience unavoidable.

I still draw because drawing slows the world down. It makes me observe edges, weight, empty space, and the small differences that disappear when I rush. It is also a useful counterweight to engineering, where everything is measured by output and iteration.

I especially love Vincent van Gogh and Claude Monet. Monet teaches me to look at light as something alive and changing. Van Gogh moves me differently. I feel sadness for his life, but also gratitude for the intensity with which he kept seeing and making. I have dreamed about Van Gogh many times. Perhaps that says less about art history than about the kind of persistence I hope to understand.

I am not trying to become a professional painter in this note. I am only recording why an ordinary practice still matters to me: it gives attention a place to rest, and it lets a person make something without needing it to be optimized.
